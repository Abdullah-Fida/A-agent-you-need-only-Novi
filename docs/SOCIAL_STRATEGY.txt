SOCIAL STRATEGY  --  Facebook, X / Twitter and Threads
================================================================================
Last updated: 2026-08-31

NO PASSWORDS OR API KEYS IN THIS FILE. It is committed to git.

Telegram is NOT part of this. It has its own schedule, its own sleep window and
its own reasons, and nothing in this file touches it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE ONE IDEA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These accounts are not the product. The website is. Every post exists to send
one more person to pressvane.com, and the link is what does that.

So two rules sit above everything else:

  * Every post carries the article link. If a link cannot be built the post is
    not sent at all -- a link-less post spends the reach and sends nobody
    anywhere, which is worse than staying quiet.

  * The post is worth reading on its own. A headline and a bare link asks for a
    click and gives nothing back, and no platform rewards that. A Facebook post
    carries the opening paragraphs of the article: someone who never clicks
    still learns what happened, and someone who does knows why it is worth the
    trip.

  * Each platform gets copy SIZED FOR IT, never one text truncated three ways.
    Facebook has room for several paragraphs, Threads for one, X for a line.
    Measured on a real article: X 140 characters, Threads 471, Facebook 846.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHEN THINGS POST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The trigger is the ARTICLE, not a clock of its own. The moment an article goes
live on the site, Facebook and X announce it. There is nothing to keep in sync,
because there is only one schedule.

Eight articles a day: six news, two explainers.

  PKT     UTC    London   New York   what
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  01:00   20:00  21:00    16:00      news        US afternoon
  04:00   23:00  00:00    19:00      news        US evening
  11:30   06:30  07:30    02:30      news        UK commute
  13:00   08:00  09:00    04:00      explainer   UK morning
  15:00   10:00  11:00    06:00      news        US wakes
  18:00   13:00  14:00    09:00      news        US PEAK
  19:30   14:30  15:30    10:30      explainer   US morning
  21:00   16:00  17:00    12:00      news        US lunch

London and New York above are summer time (BST / EDT). In winter both slide an
hour further behind PKT.

The hours look strange from Pakistan and that is the point. The readers are in
the UK and the United States, and the site was deliberately cut loose from the
Telegram schedule so it could publish through the small hours here, which is
the whole American afternoon and evening. Two of the eight slots -- 01:00 and
04:00 PKT -- sit inside the Telegram sleep window and are the best US hours of
the day.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. HOW MANY, AND THE WARM-UP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Settled speed: SIX a day on each platform, out of eight articles. And it
starts lower than that and climbs by itself:

  days 1-10    3 posts/day
  day 11+      6 posts/day

NOT EVERY POST CARRIES THE LINK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
X and Threads both demote a post for carrying an outbound link, and neither
offers a first comment to hide it in the way Facebook does. An account that
only ever posts links is throttled into a room with nobody in it -- and then
even the linked posts reach no one.

So one post in every three on those two goes out bare: headline and a fuller
extract, using the room the URL freed. That is a real post rather than an
advert, and it buys the reach that makes the LINKED posts land. Nothing is
wasted -- the profile carries the site, and a follower gained on a bare post
sees every future link.

  bluesky    LINK ALWAYS   the only one of the four with no link penalty
  facebook   LINK ALWAYS   the link is in the first comment, so it costs nothing
  twitter    2 in 3        one bare post per cycle
  threads    2 in 3        one bare post per cycle

Which post is bare is decided by the count already sent today, so it is
deterministic and testable rather than random.

Which slots survive the cap, by phase:

  PKT     New York    d1-10   d11-20   d21+
  -----------------------------------------------
  01:00   16:00         yes     yes     yes
  04:00   19:00          -       -      yes
  11:30   02:30          -       -       -
  13:00   04:00          -       -       -
  15:00   06:00          -       -      yes
  18:00   09:00         yes     yes     yes
  19:30   10:30          -      yes     yes
  21:00   12:00         yes     yes     yes
  -----------------------------------------------
  totals                 3       4       6

They are chosen by AUDIENCE, not by whichever article happened to publish
first. "The first three of the day" would hand every share to 01:00, 04:00 and
11:30 PKT -- and 11:30 PKT is half past two in the morning in New York. The
priority order lives in modules/social_syndicator.py as SLOT_PRIORITY, and a
test asserts every hour in it is an hour the website actually publishes at.

WHY SIX AND NOT EIGHT. The two left out are the 11:30 and 13:00 PKT slots --
02:30 and 04:00 in New York, the two worst hours of the eight. Those articles
lose very little: they are still written, still published, still indexed by
Google, and search is where the traffic comes from. The social post is the
smaller half of an article's value, and the two skipped would have been the
two fewest people saw.

WHY THE WEBSITE STAYS AT EIGHT. Nothing penalises a news site for publishing
often. More indexed pages is the entire growth mechanism, and Google does not
count posts per day against you the way a social platform's spam heuristics
do. The risk being managed here belongs to the accounts, not to the site, so
the site was not cut back to match.

Day one stamps itself on the first post that actually leaves, and is persisted
with the rest of the brain state, so the ramp starts on its own, cannot be
forgotten, and survives a redeploy. SOCIAL_START_DATE overrides it if needed.

4. THE SWITCHES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three, and all default OFF.

  POST /api/social/toggle             both platforms          master
  POST /api/social/facebook/toggle    Facebook only
  POST /api/social/twitter/toggle     X only

Body {"active": true} or {"active": false}. An empty body inverts, which is
only safe because NOVI always sends the explicit form -- it once read "give me
the report of the news agent" as a toggle and switched the news agent off.

Three rather than one because they fail for different reasons: an X account
gets restricted, a Facebook page is mid-rebuild. Losing one is no reason to
lose the other.

Separate from the WEBSITE switch on purpose. Turning social off does not stop
articles being written or published; it withholds the announcement and nothing
else. The master kill switch beats all of them.

8. HOW A POST IS BUILT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No model is called. The article was already written and already passed the
quality gate; paying a model to paraphrase it adds latency, cost, and one more
way to publish nonsense, for nothing.

FACEBOOK
    headline
    the opening paragraphs of the article, whole paragraphs only, up to
      about 700 characters -- headings and photo credits excluded
    three hashtags from the article's own SEO keywords
    ...and the LINK IN THE FIRST COMMENT, not in the post.

    Facebook demotes a post carrying an outbound link and does not demote a
    comment, so the reach lost by putting the URL in the body is the largest
    single cost on the page. Buffer carries it as
    metadata.facebook.firstComment. Facebook is the ONLY service with the
    field, so X and Threads keep their link in the post, where it is normal.

    Never set metadata.facebook.linkAttachment beside it: Buffer's schema
    says it is "mutually exclusive with a non-empty assets array -- input
    providing both is rejected", and every post carries the photograph.

    Flip FACEBOOK_LINK_IN_FIRST_COMMENT in social_syndicator.py to put it
    back in the body. A guard refuses to send a post whose link is in
    neither place.

THREADS
    headline
    one whole paragraph of the article, sized to what is left
    <link>
    two hashtags

    Hard limit 500, and Threads charges a URL at its REAL length -- there is
    no t.co, so a long slug genuinely costs what it reads. The body is sized
    to the space remaining AFTER the link and hashtags rather than written
    and then cut, so the post always ends on a whole sentence.

    Threads needs no metadata: every field on ThreadsPostMetadataInput is
    optional, checked against the live schema. Facebook is the only one that
    rejects a post for missing a type.

X
    headline
    the first sentence of the summary, but only if a WHOLE sentence fits
    <link>
    two hashtags

    Hard limit 280. The link is priced at 23 characters no matter how long
    it really is, because X rewrites every URL through t.co, and measuring
    the raw string wastes the budget.

The picture is the article's own hero -- the same photograph the reader sees
when they follow the link. It is already a public URL on our storage, so
nothing is re-hosted.

Hashtags come from the article's SEO keywords, and compound terms are kept
whole: "on-chain analysis" is #OnChainAnalysis. Splitting on the hyphen left
"on" looking like a stop word standing alone and published #ChainAnalysis --
a different subject, in the site's largest section.

9. THINGS LEARNED THE HARD WAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* ONE picture per article. The photograph on the page is the photograph on
  Facebook and on X: a single file in Supabase storage, one public URL, handed
  to Buffer as a link. Nothing is copied, re-uploaded or stored per platform,
  and a test asserts it.

* Buffer's free plan holds only TEN posts in the queue at once, and that ten
  is ACROSS THE WHOLE ORGANISATION, not per channel. The limit is
  OrganizationLimits.scheduledPosts, described in Buffer's own schema as "the
  maximum number of scheduled posts allowed for the organization". Six posts a
  day on two channels is twelve, so a queued setup would jam inside the first
  day -- which is exactly the state the old account was found in, sitting at
  "10 scheduled posts out of 10 allowed" and silently refusing everything.

  Posts use ShareMode "shareNow", which publishes on the spot and never enters
  the queue, so the limit does not apply to us at all. Confirmed against the
  live schema: the enum is addToQueue | customScheduled | shareNext | shareNow.
  The token cannot read organizations{limits} directly -- that scope is not
  granted to a public token -- so the number ten comes from the error message
  the API returned on the old account, not from a guess.

* CHANNEL COUNT. Buffer's published free plan is three channels. That number
  could NOT be read back from the API -- organizations{limits} returns
  FORBIDDEN to a public token -- so it comes from Buffer's pricing page, not
  from this account. You find out for certain when you connect the fourth.

  Wanted: X, Threads, Bluesky, Facebook = four. If Buffer refuses the fourth,
  the order to keep them in is Bluesky > Threads > X > Facebook, by traffic
  per post: Bluesky does not demote links at all, Threads has the best reach
  for a new account, X demotes links, and a new Facebook page has almost no
  organic reach whatsoever.

  The Pinterest agent runs on a SEPARATE Buffer account, so its channel does
  not compete for these slots.

* ANY CHANNEL IS CONFIGURATION, NOT CODE. Connect it at buffer.com, add its
  name to BUFFER_SERVICES, and SERVICE_LIMITS in social_syndicator.py sizes
  the caption. The table holds Bluesky 300, Threads and Mastodon 500,
  LinkedIn 3000, Facebook 5000 and the rest. An unknown service gets the
  TIGHTEST limit rather than the most generous -- too short is a worse post,
  too long is no post at all.

* API REQUEST limits are a SEPARATE thing from the post queue, and are the
  ones shown on buffer.com under Settings -> API:

      100 requests per 15 minutes
      500 requests per 24 hours
      10,000 requests per 30 days

  shared across every key and integration on the account. The live responses
  carry it in a header: ratelimit: "100-in-15min"; r=99; t=900.

  Measured cost of a full day, by counting the requests the code makes:

      start-up (account + channels)                     2
      six posts x four channels                        24
                                                       --
      a settled day                                    26   5.2% of 500
      a month                                         780   7.8% of 10,000
      largest burst in any 15 minutes                   4   4.0% of 100

  Running with a service enabled whose channel is NOT connected costs more,
  because the missing-channel recheck fires: 24 requests a day, still under
  5%. A test pins the whole budget so a future change that starts polling
  cannot quietly eat it.

* THE API KEY EXPIRES. The current one was created 2026-08-31 and expires
  2027-08-31. Nothing warns about this in advance -- posting will simply
  start failing with a 401 on that date. Make a new key at
  publish.buffer.com/settings/api before then and update Render.

* The token's permissions were checked against the live channel:
  allowedActions includes manageUpdates, which is the one that matters for
  publishing. viewChannel and readUpdates cover the rest of what is used.

* Facebook used to hang off the TELEGRAM fan-out. Once the website got its own
  schedule the two clocks carried different stories, so a Facebook post fired
  from the Telegram side had no article to link to and went out bare; when the
  schedules did collide, the same story went to Facebook twice. Facebook and X
  are now triggered only by the article.

* The channel list is read once at start-up. A page connected at buffer.com
  afterwards is now picked up within half an hour, without a redeploy.

* Buffer's API still calls the X channel "twitter". Both names are accepted, so
  a rename on their side cannot silently drop the channel.
