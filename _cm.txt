Stop truncating metadata mid-word; make fallback keywords searchable

Quality-tested the article pipeline against live stories. The body is strong
— 827 words, four sections, clean HTML, no narration — but the metadata had
two defects that only show up in search results.

Keywords, when the model's SEO call failed, came from splitting the headline
into single words: "notches", "best", "since", "week". Those rank for
nothing. The fallback now builds two-word search phrases, stepping past each
consumed word so "bitcoin short squeeze" yields one phrase rather than three
overlapping ones, and filters a much wider set of words that carry no search
intent.

Titles and descriptions were cut with a hard slice, so a 182-character
description ended "underscoring its sensiti" and a 74-character title ended
"Bitcoin Short Squeez". Google renders that truncation exactly as written.
Both now trim at a sentence or word boundary.

When the SEO model does answer it is already good — 'xrp price surge',
'bitcoin short squeeze', 'bitcoin liquidation cascade' from the same story —
so this only governs the fallback path and the final length enforcement.

281 tests pass.
